# Fixed-temperature heat detector plan symbol

Fire alarm plan symbol for a fixed-temperature heat detector that alarms at a set temperature.

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Heat detector, fixed temperature.

## What it represents

Fixed-temperature heat detectors alarm when the surrounding air reaches a set point, commonly around 57 to 90 degrees Celsius depending on class. Plans draw the detector box with a temperature mark or the letter H, often with a class tag.
They are used where smoke detectors would false alarm, such as kitchens, plant rooms, car parks and dusty areas. The detection is slower than smoke, so coverage spacing is tighter and the reason for choosing heat should be clear. The symbol does not give the operating temperature or class. Check the schedule, especially in hot plant rooms.

## How to read it

- Confirm the class suits the ambient temperature.
- Check spacing, which is tighter than for smoke.

## Drawing checklist

- State class or operating temperature.
- Tag loop and address.
- Note why heat detection is chosen.

## Important distinction

A rate-of-rise detector responds to a fast temperature increase; this one only to an absolute threshold.

## Facts

- Operating principle: Absolute temperature threshold
- Typical rooms: Kitchens, plant rooms, car parks
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
