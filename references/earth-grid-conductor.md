# Earth grid conductor

A buried horizontal earthing conductor forming part of a substation or plant earth grid, drawn on layout plans.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/earth-grid-conductor) · [Structured record](../entries/earth-grid-conductor.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC, IEEE / ANSI.

## What it represents

Earth grid conductors are bare horizontal conductors buried under substations and plant areas to control step and touch voltages during faults. Layout drawings show them as a distinct line style, with crossings, connections to equipment and rods marked along them.
The symbol does not show conductor size, burial depth or jointing method, which come from the earthing design. A line drawn on a plan may also not indicate whether crossings are bonded; connection marks at each crossing are needed. Above-ground risers to equipment are usually shown separately.

## How to read it

- Check crossings for bond marks.
- Look for risers to each equipment item.

## Drawing checklist

- State conductor size and burial depth.
- Mark bonded crossings.
- Show risers to equipment.

## Important distinction

An earth grid conductor is a buried horizontal conductor; an earth electrode is a rod or plate in the soil.

## Facts

- Graphic cue: Distinct line style on a site plan with bonded crossings
- Design driver: Step and touch voltage control
