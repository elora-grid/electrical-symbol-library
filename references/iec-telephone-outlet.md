# Telephone outlet plan symbol

Plan symbol for a telephone outlet where a voice service is terminated for a handset or device.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-telephone-outlet) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-telephone-outlet.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Telecommunication outlet, telephone.

## What it represents

Telephone outlets are drawn as a triangle or outlet shape tagged T or TP. In modern buildings, voice is usually carried on structured cabling, so the telephone outlet may simply be a data outlet assigned to voice, but legends still keep a separate mark for analogue lines, lift phones and alarm diallers.
Analogue lines remain common for lifts, emergency phones and some alarm communicators. The plan symbol tells the installer that a dedicated voice pair must reach this point. The symbol does not show whether the line is analogue, digital or VoIP.

## How to read it

- Check whether a dedicated analogue line is required.
- Match to the voice cabling schedule.

## Drawing checklist

- State line type.
- Tag outlet ID.
- Identify service provider demarcation.

## Important distinction

A telephone set symbol represents the handset itself; the outlet is the wall termination.

## Facts

- Service: Voice line termination
- Common dedicated uses: Lifts, emergency phones, alarm diallers
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
