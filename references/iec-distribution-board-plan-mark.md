# Distribution board plan mark

Plan mark showing the position of a distribution board or consumer unit on an installation layout.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-distribution-board-plan-mark) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-distribution-board-plan-mark.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Distribution board, installation plan.

## What it represents

On installation plans a distribution board is drawn as a rectangle against the wall, typically half filled or crossed, with its designation such as DB-1 written beside it. Home-run arrows from outlets and lights terminate in labels that refer back to this board.
The mark tells the reader where circuits originate and where the board schedule applies. In domestic work the same form is used for a consumer unit, often labelled CU. Clearance in front of the board and access requirements should be checked against the architectural plan.
The plan mark does not show the internal arrangement. The board schedule and single-line diagram show incoming supply, protective devices and circuits.

## How to read it

- Read the designation and look up the board schedule.
- Check clearances and mounting height.

## Drawing checklist

- Label the board designation.
- Reference the board schedule.
- Show clear working space.

## Important distinction

A switchboard object on a GA shows a physical lineup; the plan mark only locates a board on a building plan.

## Facts

- Graphic form: Rectangle on the wall, partly filled or crossed
- Companion document: Board schedule
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
