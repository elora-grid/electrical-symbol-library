# Junction box plan mark

Plan mark for a junction or joint box where installation cables are connected or branched.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-junction-box-plan-mark) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-junction-box-plan-mark.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Junction box, installation plan.

## What it represents

A junction box mark shows where cables are joined in an enclosure, commonly in ceiling voids for lighting circuits or above ceilings for equipment connections. Plans draw a small circle or square with a dot, sometimes labelled JB, at the location.
Access matters: joints must remain accessible for inspection unless a maintenance-free method is used, so the plan should not place a junction box above a fixed plasterboard ceiling without an access panel. The symbol helps coordinate this with architectural trades.
The mark does not show the number of terminals or cables. A junction box serving fixed equipment may also be a final connection point; check the schedule.

## How to read it

- Check access to the box above fixed ceilings.
- Look for the equipment it serves.

## Drawing checklist

- Confirm accessibility.
- State enclosure rating.
- Tag circuits passing through.

## Important distinction

A conductor junction dot on a schematic shows electrical connection; this plan mark shows a physical box.

## Facts

- Typical location: Ceiling voids, plant areas, equipment connections
- Access: Must normally remain accessible
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
