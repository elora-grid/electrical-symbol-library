# One-way light switch plan mark

Installation plan mark for a single-pole one-way switch that turns a lighting circuit on and off from one place.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-one-way-light-switch) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-one-way-light-switch.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. One-way switch, single pole.

## What it represents

The one-way switch is drawn on IEC-style plans as a small circle at the switch position with a short stroke ending in one tick. A dashed or curved switching line usually runs from the symbol to the luminaires it controls, telling the electrician which lights are switched together.
Gang count is shown by repeating the switch or by writing a number beside it, so a two-gang plate controlling two lighting groups appears as two strokes or a 2. Mounting height is normally covered by a general note rather than on each symbol.
The symbol shows a switching function and position only. It does not show the rating, the plate finish or whether the switch is single-pole or double-pole beyond the single tick convention.

## How to read it

- Follow the switching line to the luminaires; the symbol alone does not say what it controls.
- Count ticks: one tick for single-pole one-way, two ticks for double-pole.

## Drawing checklist

- Draw switching links to every controlled luminaire.
- Indicate gang count where switches share a plate.
- Reference mounting height in the general notes.

## Important distinction

A two-way switch has a second stroke so it can be paired with another switch; the one-way form controls from one point only.

## Facts

- Graphic cue: Circle with one stroke and one end tick
- Paired information: Switching line to controlled luminaires
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
