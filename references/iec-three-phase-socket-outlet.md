# Three-phase socket outlet

Installation plan mark for a polyphase socket outlet, shown with three conductor strokes on the outlet lead.

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Three-phase socket outlet.

## What it represents

A three-phase socket outlet supplies portable or semi-fixed three-phase equipment such as compressors, welders, kilns and site distribution boards. Plan symbols typically show the outlet form with three short oblique strokes, the same convention used on single-line diagrams to indicate three conductors.
Because three-phase outlets differ in pin arrangement (with or without neutral), current rating and phase rotation, the symbol is nearly always accompanied by text such as 20 A 5P or 32 A 4P. The electrician needs this to choose the accessory and to confirm phase rotation at commissioning.
The strokes indicate phase conductors only. They do not show whether neutral and protective earth are included, and they do not replace the circuit schedule entry for cable size and protective device.

## How to read it

- Count the strokes and read the pole annotation; a 4-pin and a 5-pin outlet look the same without the label.
- Check whether an isolator or interlocked switch is shown in the same position.

## Drawing checklist

- Write current rating and pin count beside the symbol.
- State whether a neutral is required.
- Record phase rotation verification in the commissioning checklist.

## Important distinction

A single-phase earthed outlet has no conductor strokes; the three strokes mark the polyphase supply.

## Facts

- Conductor indication: Three oblique strokes for the phase conductors
- Typical rating text: Current, pin count and voltage
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
