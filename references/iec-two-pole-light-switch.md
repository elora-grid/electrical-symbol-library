# Two-pole one-way switch plan mark

Plan mark for a one-way switch that breaks both line and neutral, drawn with two ticks on the stroke.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-two-pole-light-switch) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-two-pole-light-switch.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. One-way switch, two pole.

## What it represents

A two-pole one-way switch opens both poles of a single-phase circuit. The IEC plan convention adds a second tick to the one-way switch stroke, giving the reader an immediate visual cue that the switch interrupts both conductors.
Two-pole switches are specified for outdoor lighting, appliances where both poles must be broken, and some heating loads. They are also used where a switch is expected to provide functional disconnection of a fixed appliance without a separate isolator.
Two ticks do not mean the switch meets isolation requirements for maintenance. Where isolation is needed, the designer should show an isolator mark or state the isolating rating.

## How to read it

- Count the ticks carefully when the drawing is reduced in size.
- Check whether a separate isolator is also shown for the same load.

## Drawing checklist

- State whether isolation duty is intended.
- Tag the controlled load.
- Confirm current rating for heating or appliance loads.

## Important distinction

The single-tick one-way switch breaks one pole only; the two-tick form breaks both.

## Facts

- Graphic cue: Two ticks on the switch stroke
- Typical uses: Heaters, outdoor lighting, appliance control
