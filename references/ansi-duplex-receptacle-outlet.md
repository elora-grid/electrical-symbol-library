# ANSI duplex receptacle outlet plan symbol

US-style plan symbol for the standard duplex receptacle: a wall-mounted circle crossed by two parallel lines.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/ansi-duplex-receptacle-outlet) · [Structured record](../entries/ansi-duplex-receptacle-outlet.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEEE / ANSI.

Graphical convention: IEEE / ANSI. Duplex receptacle plan symbol.

## What it represents

The duplex receptacle is the default convenience outlet in US buildings and is drawn as a circle against the wall with two parallel lines through it. Because it is the most common device on a power plan, legends then modify it with letters for special types rather than inventing new shapes.
Designers use the symbol to show spacing along walls, counter receptacles and circuit groupings. Circuit numbers are written beside the symbol with home-run arrows back to the panel, and mounting heights other than standard are noted with a subscript.
The symbol does not show whether one half is switch-controlled, whether the receptacle is GFCI or AFCI protected, or its NEMA configuration. Those are added by letters or notes.

## How to read it

- Two lines through the circle mean duplex; one line means single.
- Follow the home-run arrow and circuit number to the panel schedule.

## Drawing checklist

- Tag panel and circuit.
- Note any split-wired or switched half.
- Add GFCI, WP or USB qualifiers where required.

## Important distinction

The IEC duplex receptacle form shows two outlet shapes; the US plan form uses one circle with two lines.

## Facts

- Graphic form: Circle with two parallel through-lines
- Typical rating: 15 A or 20 A, 125 V convenience outlet
- Artwork scope: Original educational illustration; the exact ANSI or IEEE graphic and compliance are not certified
