# Fused connection unit plan mark

Plan mark for a fused connection unit that feeds fixed equipment through a cartridge fuse instead of a plug.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/fused-connection-unit-plan-mark) · [Structured record](../entries/fused-connection-unit-plan-mark.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

## What it represents

A fused connection unit, often called a fused spur, connects a fixed appliance such as a towel rail, extractor fan or boiler directly to a final circuit through a small cartridge fuse. Plans commonly draw a small square with an S-shaped fuse mark or the letters FCU at the wall line, sometimes with a switch stroke for the switched type.
The symbol is common in British-style layouts on ring final circuits, where fixed loads must be fused down from the circuit rating. The reader should check the fuse rating and whether a flex outlet is fitted on the front or the cable leaves from the back of the box.
It is not a socket outlet: no plug is involved. It also does not provide isolation suitable for mechanical maintenance unless the specification says so.

## How to read it

- Read the fuse rating written beside the symbol, often 3 A or 13 A.
- Check whether the switched or unswitched version is required.

## Drawing checklist

- State fuse rating and switched or unswitched type.
- Name the fixed appliance it serves.
- Show the flex outlet or rear cable entry.

## Important distinction

A switched socket outlet accepts a plug; a fused connection unit is wired to fixed equipment.

## Facts

- Typical loads: Towel rails, extract fans, boilers, hand dryers
- Protection: Cartridge fuse in the accessory
