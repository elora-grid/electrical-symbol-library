# ANSI GFCI duplex receptacle plan symbol

US plan symbol for a duplex receptacle with built-in ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection, tagged GFCI.

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEEE / ANSI.

Graphical convention: IEEE / ANSI. GFCI duplex receptacle.

## What it represents

A GFCI receptacle includes a ground-fault circuit interrupter that trips on a small residual current, protecting people in kitchens, bathrooms, garages and outdoors. The plan uses the duplex receptacle form with GFCI or GFI written beside it.
A GFCI receptacle can also protect downstream receptacles wired from its load terminals. Plans sometimes show this by tagging only the first device and noting protected outlets, so the installer must read notes to wire the line and load terminals correctly.
The tag says protection is at the receptacle. It does not say whether a GFCI breaker in the panel would be acceptable instead, and it does not show which downstream outlets are protected unless noted.

## How to read it

- Check notes for downstream receptacles protected from the load side.
- Do not confuse GFCI with AFCI, which addresses arcing faults.

## Drawing checklist

- Identify the protecting device for every protected outlet.
- Tag weatherproof covers where outdoors.
- Keep test button accessible.

## Important distinction

An IEC RCD-protected outlet performs a similar function under a different convention and tag.
