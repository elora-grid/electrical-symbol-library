# Indicating instrument general symbol

General circle form for any indicating instrument, with a placeholder replaced by the unit or quantity measured.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/indicating-instrument-general-symbol) · [Structured record](../entries/indicating-instrument-general-symbol.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: SLD, Schematic. Reference families: IEC.

## What it represents

In IEC-style diagrams an indicating instrument is drawn as a circle, and the asterisk inside is replaced by the unit symbol or quantity, such as V, A, Hz or cos φ. The general form is useful in legends and in early design drawings when the exact instrument is not yet chosen.
Because the placeholder carries all of the meaning, an unfilled general symbol tells the reader only that some reading is shown at that point. The drawing must still state the measuring connection, scaling from instrument transformers and whether the instrument is analogue or part of a digital meter.

## How to read it

- Replace the placeholder with the actual unit before issue.
- Read the circle together with its measuring connections.

## Drawing checklist

- Fill in the measured quantity
- Show instrument transformer ratios
- List the instrument in the schedule

## Important distinction

The circle is the indicating form; recording instruments use a square and integrating instruments use a rectangle with a band.
