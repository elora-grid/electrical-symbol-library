# Coaxial cable with earthed outer conductor

A coaxial cable whose outer conductor is drawn connected to earth, recording the reference point of the RF or signal return.

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: Schematic. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Coaxial pair with earthed outer conductor.

## What it represents

This form adds an earth connection to the outer conductor of a coaxial cable. On antenna systems the outer conductor is normally bonded to the building earth where it enters, often through a lightning arrester or earthing kit, and the drawing should show that bonding explicitly.
The earth mark on the outer conductor is not a statement that the inner conductor is protected from surges. Surge protection devices on the inner conductor are separate symbols. Reading the earth location also tells you whether the coax forms an earth loop between two buildings, which matters for interference and safety.

## How to read it

- Locate the entry point bonding for antenna coax.
- Check for earth loops where both ends of the outer conductor are earthed in separate buildings.

## Drawing checklist

- Show where the outer conductor is bonded to earth.
- Identify any coaxial surge protection device separately.
- Reference the earthing conductor size for entry bonding.

## Important distinction

Earthing the outer conductor sets its reference; it is not the same as a coaxial surge protector, which clamps the inner conductor.
