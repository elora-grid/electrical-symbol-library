# Intermediate light switch plan mark

Plan mark for an intermediate switch inserted between two two-way switches to add a third control point.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-intermediate-light-switch) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-intermediate-light-switch.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Intermediate switch.

## What it represents

An intermediate switch reverses the connection of the two strapping conductors between a pair of two-way switches, so any number of intermediate switches can be added along a corridor. The plan form uses crossing strokes through the switch circle to suggest the crossover action.
On long corridors, open-plan stairs and large rooms with several doors, plans show two two-way switches at the ends and intermediate switches at the middle doors. Reading the switching lines confirms which group each set controls.
The symbol does not give contact arrangement detail or rating. Its presence also implies four-core or two-strapper wiring, which the installer must plan for.

## How to read it

- Confirm the run has two-way switches at each end.
- Count control points to check the number of intermediates.

## Drawing checklist

- Show the two end switches and every intermediate.
- Link all to the controlled luminaires.
- Allow for strapper wiring in the cable schedule.

## Important distinction

A two-way switch has a changeover contact; an intermediate switch crosses two strappers and is useless without two-way switches either side.

## Facts

- Operation: Crossover of two strapping conductors
- US equivalent term: Four-way switch
