# Ohmmeter

Indicating instrument for electrical resistance, shown as an instrument circle marked with the ohm sign.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/ohmmeter) · [Structured record](../entries/ohmmeter.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: Schematic. Reference families: IEC, IEEE / ANSI.

## What it represents

An ohmmeter indicates resistance by passing a small current from its own source and measuring the result. On permanent drawings it appears in test equipment, earthing test points and some machine monitoring panels, while on schematics for test procedures it shows where resistance is to be measured.
Because the instrument supplies its own measuring current, it must only be connected to de-energised circuits unless it is specifically designed otherwise. The symbol does not tell you the measuring voltage, range or whether a two-wire or four-wire connection is used.

## How to read it

- Confirm the measured circuit is de-energised.
- Check for four-wire connection when low resistances are measured.

## Drawing checklist

- State range
- Show test connections
- Note isolation requirements

## Important distinction

An ohmmeter measures ordinary resistance at low voltage; an insulation resistance meter applies a high test voltage to measure megohms.
