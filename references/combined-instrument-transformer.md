# Combined instrument transformer

Single unit housing a current transformer and voltage transformer for each phase, typical of MV and HV metering points.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/combined-instrument-transformer) · [Structured record](../entries/combined-instrument-transformer.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: SLD, Schematic. Reference families: IEC, Manufacturer.

## What it represents

A combined instrument transformer places a CT and a VT in one insulated housing for each phase. It saves space and foundations at outdoor HV metering points and appears in MV metering units where the supply authority needs both current and voltage for a billing meter.
On a single-line diagram the CT and VT symbols are drawn together with a boundary showing they share one housing. The boundary does not change ratios or classes, which are still stated separately for the current and voltage parts.

## How to read it

- Read the CT and VT ratings separately.
- Check sealing and access requirements for revenue metering.

## Drawing checklist

- State CT ratio and class
- State VT ratio and class
- Show sealing or metering boundary

## Important distinction

A combined instrument transformer houses a CT and VT together; separate units are drawn and specified independently.

## Facts

- Contents: CT and VT in one housing
