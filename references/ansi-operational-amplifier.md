# ANSI operational amplifier (triangle form)

Triangle op-amp symbol with minus and plus inputs on the left and the output at the apex.

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: Schematic. Reference families: IEEE / ANSI, Manufacturer.

Graphical convention: IEEE / ANSI. Triangle with inverting and non-inverting inputs on the base and output at the apex.

## What it represents

The triangular op-amp symbol is the most common analogue element in North American and manufacturer schematics. The two inputs enter the flat side, marked minus for inverting and plus for non-inverting, and the output leaves from the apex. Supply pins may be drawn on the sloping sides or omitted.
Schematics often flip the triangle vertically to simplify feedback routing, which swaps the positions of minus and plus. Always read the signs, not the positions, when tracing the circuit.
The symbol does not distinguish between general-purpose, precision, rail-to-rail or high-speed parts. The part number and its datasheet decide performance.

## How to read it

- Read the minus and plus labels, not their positions.
- Supply pins may be hidden in multi-section packages.

## Drawing checklist

- Label both inputs.
- Show supply pins or note common supply connections.
- Add the part number.

## Important distinction

A comparator uses a similar triangle but is designed for open-loop switching, often with an open-collector output.

## Facts

- Inputs: Inverting (-) and non-inverting (+)
- Common variation: Flipped vertically for routing
