# Switch with pilot light plan mark

Plan mark for a switch with an integral pilot lamp that shows when the controlled load is on.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-switch-with-pilot-light) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-switch-with-pilot-light.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Switch with pilot lamp.

## What it represents

A switch with pilot light is used where the controlled load cannot be seen from the switch position, such as an external light, a water heater or a loft. The plan form adds a small lamp mark, a crossed circle, to the switch symbol.
The pilot lamp needs a neutral at the switch in many accessory designs, so the symbol tells the electrician to bring a neutral to the switch box. It may also signal a double-pole switch for appliance control.
The lamp mark does not tell you whether it lights when the load is on or when the switch is off as a locator. The specification decides.

## How to read it

- Check whether the lamp indicates load on or acts as a locator.
- Confirm a neutral is available at the switch.

## Drawing checklist

- State lamp function.
- State pole count.
- Tag the remote load.

## Important distinction

A plain one-way switch has no lamp; this form adds visual confirmation of the load state.
