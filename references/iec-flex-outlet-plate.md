# Flex outlet plate

Plan mark for a cord or flex outlet where fixed equipment is connected directly at a wall plate.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-flex-outlet-plate) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-flex-outlet-plate.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Cord outlet.

## What it represents

A flex outlet plate terminates fixed wiring at a wall plate from which a flexible cord leaves to an appliance, such as a wall heater, hand dryer or kitchen extract hood. Plans usually show a small outlet mark with a short tail or arrow indicating the cord exit, tagged with the equipment it serves.
The symbol helps coordinate electrical and architectural trades: the appliance supplier sees where the cord must reach and the electrician knows not to install a socket. It also flags that switching or fusing must be provided elsewhere, usually by an adjacent fused connection unit or isolator.
The mark does not show any protective device. The circuit schedule or an adjacent symbol must provide switching and protection.

## How to read it

- Look for an adjacent fused connection unit or isolator that controls this plate.
- Read the equipment tag to confirm what the cord serves.

## Drawing checklist

- Tag the equipment served.
- Show the controlling switch or fuse location.
- Give mounting height relative to the appliance.

## Important distinction

A fused connection unit includes a fuse and often a switch; a flex outlet plate only provides the cord termination.

## Facts

- Contents: Cord grip and terminals only
- Usual pairing: Adjacent fused connection unit or isolator
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
