# Galvanometer

Sensitive indicating instrument used to detect very small currents or a balance point in a bridge circuit.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/galvanometer) · [Structured record](../entries/galvanometer.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: Schematic. Reference families: IEC, IEEE / ANSI.

## What it represents

A galvanometer is a very sensitive current indicator, often with a centre zero, used to detect small currents rather than measure them to a scale. It is drawn in bridge and comparison circuits, where the operator adjusts a component until the galvanometer reads zero.
On modern drawings the function is often performed by an electronic null detector, but the symbol still marks the balance point. It does not show sensitivity, damping or protection against overload, which matter because the instrument is easily damaged by currents far above its scale.

## How to read it

- Identify the two points whose balance is being detected.
- Look for protective shunts or switches around the instrument.

## Drawing checklist

- State sensitivity
- Show protective shunt
- Identify bridge arms

## Important distinction

A galvanometer detects small or zero current; an ammeter measures current to a calibrated scale.

## Facts

- Typical use: Bridge balance and null detection
- Scale: Often centre-zero
