# Floor box outlet

Plan mark for a flush floor box containing power and often data outlets in open-plan floors.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-floor-box-outlet) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-floor-box-outlet.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Floor outlet.

## What it represents

A floor box houses socket outlets and frequently data outlets below a hinged lid flush with the finished floor. Plans draw a square around the outlet mark, sometimes with a diagonal or the letters FB, and list the box contents in a schedule because each box can hold several accessory types.
Floor boxes are used in open offices, meeting rooms and lobbies where furniture is away from walls. They require coordination with the floor finish, raised-floor tiles or screed thickness, so the symbol is often cross-referenced to an architectural floor setting-out plan.
The square does not tell you how many outlets the box contains. Read the floor box schedule for power, data and AV contents and the circuit allocation of each power outlet.

## How to read it

- The square outline around the outlet is the usual floor indication.
- Expect a schedule listing box contents.

## Drawing checklist

- Reference the floor box schedule entry.
- Coordinate setting out with the floor plan grid.
- Tag power circuits and data outlet numbers.

## Important distinction

A multiple socket outlet on a wall shows a gang count; a floor box is an enclosure whose contents are scheduled separately.

## Facts

- Mounting: Flush in floor screed or raised access floor
- Contents: Power, data and AV modules per schedule
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
