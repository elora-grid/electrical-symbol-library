# ANSI/IEEE thermocouple symbol

IEEE-library thermocouple form showing the junction of two dissimilar conductors and their polarity leads.

Graphical convention: IEEE / ANSI. Autodesk IEEE-oriented Instrumentation and Sensors form: Thermocouple.

## What it represents

The thermocouple form shows two dissimilar conductors meeting at a measuring junction, with the free ends wired to an instrument or transducer. The library offers left and right handed versions so the junction can face the process side of the drawing, and the two leads must keep their polarity all the way to the cold junction.
The symbol does not identify the thermocouple type (for example K, J or T), the sheath or the extension cable. Those details matter because ordinary copper wiring between the head and the instrument introduces error unless matching extension or compensating cable is used.

## How to read it

- Check the thermocouple type in the instrument schedule.
- Verify extension cable type and polarity from the head to the input.

## Drawing checklist

- State thermocouple type
- Mark lead polarity
- Specify extension or compensating cable

## Important distinction

A thermocouple generates a small voltage from a temperature difference; an RTD changes resistance and needs an excitation current.

- Convention evidence: Autodesk IEEE Symbol Preview, Instrumentation and Sensors
