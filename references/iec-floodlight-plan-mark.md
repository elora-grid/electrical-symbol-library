# Floodlight plan mark

Plan mark for a floodlight that lights an area, drawn as a luminaire with a widening beam.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-floodlight-plan-mark) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-floodlight-plan-mark.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Floodlight.

## What it represents

Floodlights for yards, sports areas, facades and car parks are drawn as a luminaire mark with diverging beam lines, showing a wide spread. The aiming direction is shown by the beam and mounting height is noted.
Floodlights are typically controlled by a photocell, time switch or contactor because the load is large. The plan symbol often sits on a building wall or a pole and is linked to that control scheme. Beam lines are indicative. Photometric calculations, not the symbol, confirm coverage.

## How to read it

- Note aiming direction and mounting height.
- Find the control scheme.

## Drawing checklist

- State mounting height and tilt.
- Show controls.
- Reference photometric design.

## Important distinction

A spotlight has a single narrow beam arrow; a floodlight shows a diverging beam.

## Facts

- Graphic cue: Diverging beam lines
- Typical control: Photocell and contactor
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
