# Room thermostat plan mark

Plan mark for a wall-mounted room thermostat controlling heating or cooling equipment.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-room-thermostat-plan-mark) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-room-thermostat-plan-mark.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Thermostat.

## What it represents

A room thermostat is drawn as a small circle or square with a temperature mark, a theta or the letter T, at its wall position. A control link connects it to the heater, valve or air-conditioning unit it controls.
Its position affects comfort: it should not be near heat sources, in direct sunlight or on an external wall. The plan gives the designer a chance to check this, and the mounting height is usually stated in notes.
It does not show whether the thermostat switches mains directly or provides an extra-low-voltage control signal. Wiring type follows the controlled equipment.

## How to read it

- Check its position relative to heat sources.
- Identify what it controls.

## Drawing checklist

- Link to controlled equipment.
- State control voltage.
- Give mounting height.

## Important distinction

A schematic temperature switch shows a contact; the plan mark locates the room control.

## Facts

- Graphic cue: Circle with temperature symbol
- Placement check: Away from heat sources
