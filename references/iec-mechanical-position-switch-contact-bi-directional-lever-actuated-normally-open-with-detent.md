## What it represents

A mechanically operated position switch changes its contact state when travel, a cam or a lever reaches the documented operating condition. The actuator mark explains how movement reaches the switch; the electrical contact still has to be read in its stated unoperated position. This entry identifies the bi-directional lever actuated - normally open with detent form in the publisher's IEC-context family.
A moving carriage may operate the switch near the end of travel, or an operator may move a multidirectional lever. Identify the actual direction or target position on the drawing and connect the switch tag to the corresponding mechanical detail. The normally-open designation starts with a non-conducting output or open contact in the documented reference condition; operation changes that state. The detent retains the lever position after actuation; a retained indication and a spring-return position input require different operating logic.
Two operating directions are represented. Identify the direction-to-contact mapping in the mechanical detail rather than assuming one signal applies to every movement. Detents retain an operating position and are different from a spring-return mechanism. The schematic does not supply operating travel, overtravel, contact force or a safety performance level; use the selected switch data for those properties.
