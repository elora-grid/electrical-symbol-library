# Switched socket outlet

Plan mark for a socket outlet with its own integral switch that controls the supply to the plug.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-switched-socket-outlet) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-switched-socket-outlet.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Switched socket outlet.

## What it represents

A switched socket outlet combines the outlet and a single-pole or double-pole switch in one accessory plate. On the plan the outlet half circle carries a short switch stroke with a tick, borrowed from the light-switch form, showing that the user can interrupt supply at the outlet without removing the plug.
Switched outlets are the norm in Australian and British domestic work and common in kitchens, offices and plant rooms elsewhere. The symbol helps the electrician choose the correct accessory and helps the estimator count switched against unswitched outlets, which differ in cost and in the depth of box required.
The mark does not say whether the switch breaks one pole or both, and it does not make the outlet suitable for isolation of fixed equipment. Pole count and any isolation duty should be stated in the specification.

## How to read it

- The switch stroke is attached to the outlet, not drawn as a separate switch symbol nearby.
- Count switched and unswitched outlets separately when taking off quantities.

## Drawing checklist

- Specify single-pole or double-pole switching.
- Identify outlets that must be unswitched, such as refrigerator or server outlets.
- Keep circuit tags and earthing information on the symbol.

## Important distinction

A socket outlet with interlocked switch prevents plug insertion or withdrawal while energised; a plain switched outlet only lets the user switch the supply.

## Facts

- Drawing role: Outlet with local on/off control
- Quantity note: Counted separately from unswitched outlets
