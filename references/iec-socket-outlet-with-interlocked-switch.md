# Socket outlet with interlocked switch

Plan mark for an outlet whose switch is mechanically interlocked so a plug cannot be inserted or withdrawn while live.

Graphical convention: IEC. Socket outlet with interlocked switch.

## What it represents

Interlocked socket outlets are used for industrial plugs, welding outlets, portable plant supplies and higher-current three-phase outlets. The interlock forces the user to switch off before the plug can be removed or inserted, which prevents drawing an arc at the pins. The plan mark adds a switch stroke and a small interlock bar to the outlet form.
On industrial layouts the symbol is often paired with a current rating and pole count written beside it, for example 32 A 5-pin. The estimator should expect a heavier enclosure, sometimes with an integral fuse or circuit breaker, and the installer should expect a surface-mounted accessory rather than a flush plate.
The interlock mark does not confirm the presence of overcurrent or residual-current protection inside the unit. Check the schedule for combination units that include a breaker or RCD.

## How to read it

- Check the rating text: interlocked outlets are usually specified by current, poles and IP rating together.
- Do not confuse the interlock bar with the protective earth bar on a domestic outlet.

## Drawing checklist

- State current rating, pole count and plug standard.
- State whether overcurrent or residual-current protection is integral.
- Note IP rating and mounting surface.

## Important distinction

A simple switched outlet allows the plug to be withdrawn while energised; the interlocked form mechanically prevents it.

## Facts

- Typical location: Workshops, plant rooms and outdoor plant supply points
- Added information: Plug cannot move while the switch is on
- Exact standard graphic: Not reproduced here; confirm the form against IEC 60617 and the project legend
