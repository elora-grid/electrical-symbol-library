# Coaxial cable

A centre conductor drawn within a concentric outer conductor, used for RF, video and some high-frequency measurement leads.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/iec-coaxial-cable) · [Structured record](../entries/iec-coaxial-cable.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: Schematic. Reference families: IEC.

Graphical convention: IEC. Coaxial pair form.

## What it represents

The coaxial cable symbol shows an inner conductor surrounded by a concentric outer conductor that forms the return path. Unlike a screened conductor, the outer conductor of a coaxial cable is part of the signal circuit and sets a defined characteristic impedance. It appears on antenna feeds, GPS time-sync leads to protection relays, video circuits and some partial-discharge sensor connections.
The symbol does not give the impedance, attenuation or connector type. A 50 ohm and a 75 ohm cable look identical on the drawing, so the cable schedule must identify the type. Where the outer conductor is earthed, show that separately rather than assuming the reader knows the earthing arrangement.

## How to read it

- Check the cable type and impedance against the equipment at both ends.
- Look for connector designations at each end of the coax run.

## Drawing checklist

- State characteristic impedance or cable type.
- Name the connector at each end.
- Show earthing of the outer conductor where applicable.

## Important distinction

In coaxial cable the outer conductor carries return current; in a screened conductor the screen only intercepts interference.

## Facts

- Graphic cue: Centre line with a concentric outer outline extended to both ends
- Typical use: GPS antenna leads, RF and video circuits
