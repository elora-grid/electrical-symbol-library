# ANSI ceiling lighting outlet plan symbol

US plan symbol for a ceiling lighting outlet: a plain circle at the fixture position, tagged with the fixture type.

[Canonical reference](https://eloragrid.ai/symbols/ansi-ceiling-lighting-outlet) · [Structured record](../entries/ansi-ceiling-lighting-outlet.json) · [All references](README.md)

Updated: 2026-10-28. Drawing use: GA. Reference families: IEEE / ANSI.

Graphical convention: IEEE / ANSI. Ceiling lighting outlet.

## What it represents

In US lighting plans a ceiling lighting outlet or surface fixture is drawn as a circle, often with a letter inside or beside it keyed to the fixture schedule. Lowercase letters link fixtures to the switches that control them.
Rectangular fixtures are drawn to scale as rectangles instead, so the circle typically means a round fixture or a generic outlet box. Emergency fixtures are often shown half filled. The circle does not show mounting type, which comes from the schedule.

## How to read it

- Read the fixture letter and switch letter.
- Check for half-filled circles for emergency fixtures.

## Drawing checklist

- Key fixtures to the schedule.
- Key switches with lowercase letters.
- Mark emergency fixtures.

## Important distinction

The IEC luminaire symbol is a crossed circle; the US outlet symbol is a plain circle.

## Facts

- Graphic form: Plain circle, lettered
- Emergency cue: Half-filled circle in many legends
